# Changelog

Each release of fxbot, newest first. A release that adds migrations, bumps libfx, or needs a step by hand opens with **Before upgrading**. Lines for the next release collect under Unreleased. GitHub Releases and Settings show a short version of each section.

## Unreleased

**Before upgrading.** Migration `0016_effort` adds an `effort` column to `profiles`, for how hard a bot's model thinks, and to `plugin_sessions`, for a plugin's session that thinks at its own level. Every bot starts at its model's own default, as before. Migration `0017_team_memories` lets a memory have no bot, which makes it the team's, and adds `by_bot`, the bot that saved one. Every memory there is stays its bot's own. Migration `0018_idle_compaction` adds idle compaction's settings to `settings`: on, after 30 minutes, with each bot's own model. Remove the fresh plugin from **Plugins**, where it shows **Unsupported**: `/fresh` is built in now. Then update Subagents from **Plugins**, for its `thinking` setting, and Chat rooms, for a room's memories.

- **Choose how hard a bot thinks.** **Thinking**, under **Model** on a bot's edit screen, offers the levels its model has, such as Low, Medium, High, Extra high, and Max, or the model's own default. fxbot reads each model's levels from where its provider lists them: AI Gateway's catalog, ChatGPT's and Grok's lists, models.dev, OpenRouter, Ollama, and LM Studio. A level the model doesn't offer runs as the nearest lower one, never Off, and the screen says so. The level reaches the model through AI Gateway, Vercel, ChatGPT, and Grok now, and through every other provider with the next libfx release. See [Choose how hard a bot thinks](https://fxbot.sh/docs/how-to/choose-how-hard-a-bot-thinks/).
- **Subagents think at a level of their own,** with Subagents 0.2.0: `thinking` in **Settings** › **Subagents** is `same`, the bot's level, or one such as `high`.
- **Team memories.** Notes every one of your bots reads, such as who you are and how you like things done, in **Settings** › **Memory**, where you add, edit, search, and delete them. A bot saves one with `memory_save` and `team` when you ask it to remember something for all your bots, and the list says which bot saved each one. **Share with every bot**, beside each of a bot's own memories, moves one to the team. They go in every bot's instructions after its own, and `memory_search` finds them. See [Team memories](https://fxbot.sh/docs/explanation/bots-and-chats/#team-memories).
- **`/fresh` is built in.** It works in every bot's chat, as the fresh plugin's did, with nothing to add, and the plugin leaves fxbot's list and **Essentials**. See [Start a chat over with /fresh](https://fxbot.sh/docs/how-to/start-fresh/).
- **Idle compaction.** A long chat that sits with no message for 30 minutes is summarized while you're away, so your next message starts fast: the bot reads the summary and the last messages, not the whole chat again. The chat shows **Compacted while idle**, which opens to the summary, and the earlier messages stay above it. **Idle compaction** in **Settings** › **General** sets the time, or **Off**, and **Summarize with** picks a cheaper model to write the summary. A message you send meanwhile calls it off. See [Idle compaction](https://fxbot.sh/docs/explanation/bots-and-chats/#idle-compaction).
- **An empty summary is no summary.** libfx answers a request that gets no text with "Done.", which a provider switch or `/fresh` took as the chat's summary. Now it fails as any summary that didn't come back does: a switch summarizes from the stored messages instead, and `/fresh` says the chat couldn't be summarized.
- **A room's own memories,** with Chat rooms 0.4.0: **Memories**, at the top of a room, keeps notes every bot in that room reads there, such as what the room is for and what was decided. The room's bots save them too, with `chat-rooms_remember`. See [Give a room memories](https://fxbot.sh/docs/how-to/talk-in-a-room/#give-a-room-memories).
- **Send PDFs to a bot.** A PDF of up to 8 MiB attaches as an image or a text file does. The bot reads the text fxbot finds in it, page by page: up to 60,000 characters in your message, and the rest from the library, where `library_read` now reads a long document in parts. A PDF of scanned pages says it has no text. The library keeps the PDF itself, which downloads as it is, and its preview shows the text the bot reads. Search finds a PDF by its words.
- **Bots with a soul.** The new **soul** plugin, in **Essentials**, gives each bot a soul: a few lines, in its own words, of who it is, how it sounds, and how it helps you. The first time you open a new bot's chat, it speaks first: it guesses who it might be from its name, asks what you'd like help with, and writes its soul from your answers. It rewrites it when you ask, in its own chat only, and **Soul** on its edit screen lets you edit it, with every version to go back to. Its skill, `import-profile`, brings a soul and memories over from a Hermes Agent profile or an OpenClaw agent, from files you send, or from Grok Bot, ChatGPT, or Claude through a prompt you paste there. Joining **Essentials** doesn't add it to an install that has fxbot already: add it from **Plugins**. See [Give a bot a soul](https://fxbot.sh/docs/how-to/give-a-bot-a-soul/).
- **For plugins:** an `app.chat` part gets `reload()`, which reads the bot again, so the chat follows a session the plugin started. `fx.chats.newSession(bot, { effort })` gives a session of the plugin's own a thinking level in place of the bot's, and `fx.library.save` takes a PDF. A `Memory` has `team` and `savedByBot`; `fx.memories` still reaches only a bot's own. A message hook's `startOver` doesn't count when the message began with `/fresh`, which fxbot runs itself.
- **No more box around a moving bot in Firefox.** With hardware acceleration on, a bot that leaned after the pointer, such as the one open in the sidebar, could show thin straight lines along its right and bottom edges. The home page's bots on fxbot.sh had them too ([#16](https://github.com/iammrduncan/fxbot/issues/16)).

## 0.9.0 (2026-11-10)

**Before upgrading.** Migration `0015_session_labels` adds a `label` column to `plugin_sessions`, for the name a plugin gives a session of its own. Every session you have carries on as it is. Then update Computer from **Plugins**, if you have it.

- **Web pairing leaves Essentials.** Add it from **Plugins** when you want your fxbot on your phone. An install that has it keeps it.
- **Subagents.** The new **subagents** plugin, in **Essentials**, lets a bot hand work to helpers that run in the background while it carries on talking with you. Each is the bot itself, run again on one task, with its tools, computer, skills, and memories, but not its chat. The subagents one turn starts report back together, in one message from **Subagents**, once the last has ended, and the bot tells you what they found. Each has a name of its own, in its own color in the bot's panel, which lists them, working and recent, and each opens to its thread, live, with **Stop**. A subagent can tell the bot's chat what can't wait, and the bot can message a working subagent, by name. With the plugin on, a bot hands work that takes a lot of digging to subagents by itself, and tells you it'll come back with the results. A subagent can't start one of its own, and leaves memories, messages to other bots, routines, and asking you to the bot's chat. Joining **Essentials** doesn't add it to an install that has fxbot already: add it from **Plugins**. See [Hand work to subagents](https://fxbot.sh/docs/how-to/hand-work-to-subagents/).
- **Diagrams in chat.** The new **mermaid** plugin draws the ```` ```mermaid ```` code blocks bots write as diagrams, in replies, Markdown pages, rooms, and the library, in the app's own colors and font, light and dark. A diagram grows as the bot writes it, and one with a mistake shows its code and what's wrong. Bots get a skill, and `mermaid_check` to try a diagram before they send it. It draws with [Pele](https://pele.run), Obsidian's Mermaid renderer, loaded with the first diagram. Add it from **Plugins**. See [Draw diagrams in chat](https://fxbot.sh/docs/how-to/draw-diagrams-in-chat/) ([#9](https://github.com/iammrduncan/fxbot/issues/9)).
- **A bot's subagents work on its computer together,** with Computer 0.4.0: each has its name and arrow on the computer's screen, its own browser tab with the bot's sign-ins, and turns at the desktop and the terminal, as a room's bots take turns.
- **Bots write pages without being asked.** With Pages 0.3.0, a long answer, such as a report, a comparison, research findings, or a plan, comes as a page, and the bot says in a sentence or two what it covers. What a few sentences answer stays in the chat, and so does anything you ask to have there.
- **fxbot's plugins teach bots how to use them** ([#14](https://github.com/iammrduncan/fxbot/issues/14)). Pages 0.3.0, Subagents, Routines 0.2.0, and Messages 0.2.0 each carry a skill now, as Computer and Chat rooms do: when to use the plugin, and how to use it well, which a bot reads when a task calls for it. Pages' has the rules for an HTML page and one to start from, Subagents' how to split work and write a task, Routines' schedules to copy, and Messages' how replies go. Computer's `browser` and `desktop` skills now hold the commands a bot needs, where they sent it to read each tool's own guide first. Update them from **Plugins**.
- **A message you haven't sent stays in its bot's box** when you go to another screen, reload, or close the app, until you send it or clear it. Each bot keeps its own, in that browser ([#13](https://github.com/iammrduncan/fxbot/issues/13)).
- **For plugins:** `fx.chats.newSession(bot, { label })` names a session of the plugin's own, and each of its tool calls, and `tool.before` and `tool.after`, get `label`, or null in a bot's own chat. `fx.chats.send` with `steer` now works in a session of the plugin's own too, so a message joins a turn running there. On the page, `app.codeBlock(language, Component)` draws a language's fenced code blocks wherever the app shows Markdown, `ui.Streamdown` included.
- **Each of fxbot's plugins links to its docs.** Its page in **Plugins**, its tab in **Settings**, and its page in the [plugin directory](https://fxbot.sh/plugins/) have **Docs**, which opens its guide on fxbot.sh. The links come with fxbot's plugin list, which fxbot reads hourly, so a new guide needs no release. For the SDK, a `PackagePlugin` and a `PackageCard` carry `docs`, a path on fxbot.sh, when the list has one.

## 0.8.0 (2026-11-10)

**Before upgrading.** Migration `0014_provider_fields` adds `fields` and `model_ids` columns to `providers`, for a provider's region or account and the models you type for it. Every provider you have carries on as it is. The update to 0.8.0 runs 0.7.0's `fxbot` command, which leaves 0.7.0's image: `sudo podman rmi ghcr.io/iammrduncan/fxbot:0.7.0`, or `sudo docker rmi` on Docker, takes it away. With the computer, run `sudo fxbot computer on` once after updating: it moves the computer's lines out of `compose.override.yaml`, into a file of their own.

- **A new page for adding a provider.** **Add provider**, in **Settings** › **Providers**, opens a page of providers, each with its logo, sign-ins first, then keys, clouds, and servers on your own machine, with a search over about 200 providers. First-run setup shows the same page, sign-ins first.
- **Sign in to OpenRouter and OrcaRouter, with nothing to paste.** OpenRouter's sign-in comes back to fxbot's own page, or shows a code to paste where it can't. OrcaRouter's is a device code. Each makes a key on your account, which fxbot keeps sealed. Hugging Face signs in by device code too, through fxbot's own app there. Requests to OpenRouter now name fxbot as the app that sent them.
- **A device code shows a QR of its link,** and buttons to copy the code and the link, for an account that lives on your phone.
- **Each key, walked through.** A card for each of 23 gateways and labs links straight to the page where its keys are made, says what to pick there, and what it costs to start, then checks the key for real. Groq, Anthropic, Google Gemini, DeepSeek, Z.ai, Qwen, Kimi, MiniMax, xAI, Mistral, Together, Fireworks, DeepInfra, Cerebras, NVIDIA, Hugging Face, and Nous Portal are new.
- **Clouds.** Vertex AI with a service account's file, from which fxbot makes its own short-lived tokens, AWS Bedrock with an API key and a region, Azure OpenAI with a resource and its deployments, and Cloudflare Workers AI with a token and an account.
- **More servers on your own machine.** llama.cpp, vLLM, and LiteLLM join Ollama and LM Studio, each with its usual address filled in, and one that's running is marked as found.
- **Custom** takes any server that speaks OpenAI's Chat Completions, as Other server did, and model ids for a server that lists none.
- **Models say what they can do.** The model list comes from the provider itself, with models.dev's context window, tool use, and images shown for each model, and a good first model filled in after you connect.
- **The list of providers updates without a release.** fxbot reads it hourly from fxbot.sh, as it does the plugin list, so a new gateway, or a key page that moved, needs no update.
- **Skip for now skips one step** of first-run setup, where it ended the whole setup: plugins go on to the provider, and the provider to your first bot. On the bot step, it ends setup.
- **A refused key says which provider, and what to do.** A key refused when you add it says the status and what it most likely means. A chat whose provider refuses its key says "Groq rejected this provider's key. Replace it in Settings › Providers.", where it said "Stopped: refused".
- **For the SDK:** `providers.catalog()` and `providers.local()` list what the page offers, and the local servers that answer. `providers.add` takes `fields`, `serviceAccount`, and `modelIds`, and a provider's `kind` is any entry's id. A `Provider` gains `label`, `connect`, `docs`, `fields`, and `modelIds`. A `SignIn` gains `way` and `url`, its `verificationUrl` and `userCode` are there only for a device code, and `signIns.code` finishes one that shows a code. A `Provider`'s `kind` is a `string`. `createFxbot` takes `hfClientId`, and the server `FXBOT_HF_CLIENT_ID`.
- **Your fxbot on your phone, from anywhere.** The new **web-pairing** plugin, in **Essentials**, opens your own fxbot at [web.fxbot.sh](https://web.fxbot.sh), on a phone or any computer, over a WebRTC connection straight to it. fxbot.sh passes only the sealed handshake, and your fxbot's own page, plugins and all, comes from your fxbot. It connects to nothing until you pair a device.
- **Pair a device once.** **Settings** › **Web pairing** › **Pair a device** shows a code and a QR code. Type the code on the device, check both screens show the same six digits, and approve. A device stays paired until you remove it, reconnects by itself, and can pair another from anywhere. An opt-in passkey pairs a wiped one again with no other device.
- **It says what will connect.** **Reachability** checks your fxbot's network in advance: UDP, its NAT, a mapped port, IPv6, and a container. A device that can't connect says why after 15 seconds, and links to the fix. Cloudflare's STUN finds the way by default; add STUN and TURN servers of your own, in your order. fxbot maps its UDP ports on the router by NAT-PMP or UPnP where it can.
- **New guides** for reaching fxbot from your phone, pairing another device, what to do when a device can't connect, forwarding a port, WireGuard, and running your own TURN server, STUN server, or relay, with how it all works.
- **For plugins:** `fx.secrets` keeps values a plugin makes itself, such as keys of its own, sealed beside its settings, and `fx.localUrl` is the server's own address, for requests to fxbot's own API.
- **fxbot installs on more of Linux.** `setup.sh` installs through apt, dnf, zypper, or pacman, so Fedora, AlmaLinux and the other RHEL rebuilds, openSUSE Tumbleweed and Leap, and Arch join Debian and Ubuntu, with the distros built on them. Arch's support builds on [PR #11](https://github.com/iammrduncan/fxbot/pull/11), thanks to Olen Thompson. On Fedora, RHEL, and openSUSE, SELinux and firewalld stay on: fxbot's folders are labelled for containers, and podman's DNS rules come back after a firewalld reload.
- **`setup.sh` checks before it changes anything.** It stops, with what to do, on macOS or Windows, without systemd, on a CPU fxbot has no image for, with too little disk, when another program has the port, when Docker is a snap, or when apt would remove a package to install one. With under 1 GB of memory free, it says so and carries on. A new page, [Fix an install that stopped](https://fxbot.sh/docs/how-to/fix-an-install/), has each stop and its fix.
- **It takes the engine that's there.** A Docker that's running now wins over an installed podman, and `FXBOT_ENGINE` names one. With no engine, it installs podman 4.9 or newer, or Docker's own `docker-ce` where the distribution's podman is older, as on Debian 12 and Ubuntu 22.04, and never Ubuntu's `docker.io` again. It shows the images downloading, and lists each package it installs in `/etc/fxbot/packages`.
- **Another port than 8787.** `curl -fsSL https://fxbot.sh/setup.sh | sudo FXBOT_PORT=8780 bash` installs on 8780, the same port inside the app's container as out.
- **The computer runs on every engine.** Its engine is rootless podman wherever the distribution has 4.9 or newer, beside whichever engine fxbot runs on, so Ubuntu 24.04 with `docker.io` gets a computer, where its setup stopped before. Where podman is older, it's the machine's own Docker, run rootless. A computer already set up keeps its engine. It stops, changing nothing, on cgroups v1 or without user namespaces. Its compose file is its own now, `compose.computer.yaml`, so `compose.override.yaml` is yours alone. The computer plugin goes to 0.3.2.
- **The computer's setup works without systemd-resolved,** as on a plain Debian, where it stopped on reading resolved's file. And a computer home that's a disk of its own is the user's, and emptied on `--remove`.
- **`sudo fxbot backup`** writes the database and the plugins' files into a folder `fxbot restore` takes. [Back up fxbot](https://fxbot.sh/docs/how-to/back-up/) uses it, and has how to move to a new machine.
- **`sudo fxbot uninstall`** takes fxbot off the machine, after one question: its containers, volumes, and images, the computer, the units, and the command. It keeps a backup, the key, and the `fxbot` user, so you can install again on them, unless you add `--purge`. It names the packages `setup.sh` installed, with the command to remove them. See [Uninstall fxbot](https://fxbot.sh/docs/how-to/uninstall/).
- **On podman with ufw,** each start lets the containers' DNS in on their bridge, so an install behind ufw's default deny no longer fails on `EAI_AGAIN db`. The update from 0.7.0 starts fxbot with 0.7.0's command, so run `sudo fxbot up` once after it.
- **Updates from this release on take the old release's images away** once the new one runs, and a GPU trial that finds no GPU takes its 4 GB image away too.
- **`fxbot status` says whether fxbot answers,** and `fxbot logs` ends when it's piped, as when an agent runs it.
- **A wrong OpenRouter key is refused when you add it.** OpenRouter's model list answers anyone, so fxbot took any key there, and the key failed only at the first message. fxbot now checks it where OpenRouter refuses a bad one.

## 0.7.0 (2026-11-09)

**Before upgrading.** Migration `0011_onboarding` adds `onboarding_step` and `onboarded_at` columns to `settings`. An install with a bot, a plugin, or a provider is marked done, so it never sees first-run setup. Migration `0012_looks` adds `mood`, `head`, `face`, `outfit`, and `tint` columns to `profiles`. Every bot keeps its shape and its color, drawn the new way. Migration `0013_bot_order` adds a `position` column to `profiles`, for the order you drag bots into. Until you do, they keep the order they had. Then update your plugins from **Plugins**: each of fxbot's shows **Update available** once. Chat rooms 0.3.1 adds a `position` column to its rooms, and Messages 0.1.1 an `asked` column to its conversations, when they update.

- **Bots have a new look.** Big dark eyes, soft color, and more shapes: the fourteen you know and eighteen more, such as toast, cat, frog, and ghost.
- **A feeling, and things to wear.** Give a bot a feeling, from bright to doubtful, and something for its head, its face, and its body, each cut to fit its shape, in the bot's own color, black, or white. A blazer on a heart sits as well as a blazer on a circle.
- **A bot shows what it's doing.** It focuses and hops while it works, looks at you and bobs while a call waits on an answer, smiles when it finishes, and sleeps when it has no provider. Its head seems to turn as it watches the pointer, and a click gets a squish and a smile. With reduced motion on, it holds still.
- **A new bot screen.** Pick from tabs for **Shape**, **Color**, **Feeling**, **Head**, **Face**, and **Body**, with each choice drawn on your bot, or tap **Surprise me**. It's the same in first-run setup, on a phone the bot and the tabs stay in view as you scroll, and **Change look** on a bot's edit page opens it with a **Save look** of its own.
- **A bot's edit page keeps Save in view,** however long the page gets.
- **A menu on each bot.** Right-click a bot in the sidebar, or hold it on a phone, to edit it, pin or unpin it, make it the default bot, or delete it. The default bot wears a small star in the sidebar.
- **Your own order for bots and rooms.** Drag one in the sidebar to move it, with a mouse, or with a finger after a moment's hold, so a swipe still scrolls. Pinned bots move among themselves. Chat rooms 0.3.1 orders rooms the same way.
- **For plugins:** a page's `ui` has `useReorder`, the app's drag to reorder a list.
- **Chat rooms 0.3.1, Messages 0.1.1, and Permissions 0.1.1** show each bot's whole look, and so does Pages. Update them from **Plugins**.
- **A bot knows when a request is yours.** When you ask one bot for something and it asks another, the other gets your own words with the message, quoted by fxbot from your chat rather than from the bot's message, and so does any bot it asks in turn. It can tell the work is yours, and that what it's asked fits what you said. What it would ask you about first still needs your OK. Messages 0.1.1 does this on 0.6.0 as well.
- **Auto asks about what's risky, not everyday work.** Its reviewer allows editing files, builds, tests, installs, starting services, and the like, whether or not you asked for that step. It asks you before what's risky, unless you plainly asked for it: deleting what can't be put back, changing something outside the bot's own computer, such as a remote API, a deploy, or a push, touching secrets, weakening security, and spending money. It also counts what you asked another bot that passed the work on, which needs fxbot 0.7.0. In Permissions 0.1.1.
- **For plugins:** `fx.chats.send` takes `asked`, the owner's own words a message carries on, and the `message`, `turn.start`, and `turn.end` hooks get it.
- **The message box is whole when a chat opens.** With the Messages plugin, a chat opened from the sidebar showed its box's hint and cursor too low, cut off at the bottom, until you typed. Messages 0.1.1 fixes it on 0.6.0 as well.
- **For plugins:** pass a whole bot to `Blob`, as in `<ui.Blob {...bot} size={24} />`, and it shows what the bot wears and is doing, and an older fxbot ignores what it doesn't know. `waiting` on a bot is true while a `tool.before` hook's `{ wait }` holds one of its calls.
- **For the SDK:** a `Profile` gains `mood`, `head`, `face`, `outfit`, `tint`, and `waiting`, and `moods`, `heads`, `faces`, `outfits`, and `tints` list what each takes. `shapes` has 32, and `colors` keeps its names with softer values.
- **Pages.** A bot writes a report, a guide, or a plan to read outside the chat, from the new `pages` plugin, in **Essentials**. A card in its chat opens the page, and the library keeps it, with every version. When a page needs more than text, such as a chart with filters, the bot writes it in HTML, in a frame of its own. A task list has boxes you tick, and the bot sees them. An HTML page saves what you do on it, a routine can send it new numbers without rewriting it, once you allow it, and a button on a page can draft a message to the bot.
- **First-run setup.** A new install walks through plugins, a provider and a model, and a first bot, then opens its chat. It saves each step, so a restart comes back to it, and **Skip for now** is on every step.
- **Essentials.** A pack of what most people want from fxbot, at the top of **Browse**, with **Add all**, or **Add the other** few you don't have. Each plugin in it stays its own.
- **fxbot knows which plugins can run on it.** A plugin that needs a newer fxbot says so on its page, and **Add** stays off. A newer version that needs a newer fxbot shows **Needs fxbot** in place of **Update available**, and the one you have keeps running.
- **Unsupported.** When a release breaks something a plugin uses, the plugin doesn't run, its tab says why and how to fix it, and the rest of fxbot starts as usual. Its files, settings, and tables are kept, and an update that fits brings it back.
- **Settings warns before an update,** naming the plugins that wouldn't run on the new release.
- **A plugin's page that fails to load says so** on its tab, where before only the browser's console knew.
- **Each of fxbot's plugins says the oldest fxbot it runs on,** so they show **Update available** once, with nothing to do but update.
- **For plugins:** `fx.library.replace` changes a document in place, unless another bot holds it too or the owner sent it, the `library.removed` hook says an item went, and `app.libraryItem` is a place in an item's preview. A plugin can be named `pages`, though not have a route at its root. See the [plugin API](https://fxbot.sh/sdk/reference/plugin-api/).
- **For the SDK:** `profiles.order(ids)` and `PUT /api/profiles/order` set the sidebar's order. `Settings` gains `onboarded` and `onboardingStep`, and `UpdateStatus` gains `wontRun`, from `plugins.wontRun(next)`. A plugin gains `unsupported`, its mark can be `{ kind: "needs" }`, a plugin's preview `unfit` and `needsFxbot`, and a package search `essentials`, with a `note` on each card.
- **For plugins:** `minFxbot` in `plugin.json` is the oldest fxbot a plugin runs on, and fxbot publishes what each release broke in [plugin-breaks.json](https://fxbot.sh/plugin-breaks.json). See [Which fxbot it runs on](https://fxbot.sh/sdk/reference/plugin-api/#which-fxbot-it-runs-on). It adds no migration.

## 0.6.0 (2026-11-06)

**Before upgrading.** Migrations `0009_time_zone` and `0010_turns_at_once` add `time_zone` and `turns_at_once` columns to `settings`. A time zone set in dreams moves to **Settings** › **General**: the first browser to open fxbot sets it there, so check it if yours should be another.

- **Choose what each bot can do alone,** from the new permissions plugin, on fxbot's list. Each bot has a mode: **Ask** asks you before anything but a safe tool, **Auto** has a reviewer model check risky calls against what you said, and **Yolo** runs everything. A call that needs you shows a card above the message box, with the exact action, and **Approve once**, **Always allow**, and **Deny**. A card from work you didn't start expires after 30 minutes, and the action doesn't happen. The reviewer can be another provider and model. It adds no core migration. See [Choose what a bot can do alone](https://fxbot.sh/docs/how-to/choose-what-a-bot-can-do-alone/).
- **Removing a provider stops what's running on it.** A turn stuck on a provider could go on calling it with the old key until fxbot restarted, and **Remove** could hang. Now it ends those turns first, deleting a bot ends all its turns, in rooms and routines too, and a key that's replaced or removed stops working at once.
- **A bot stuck on a provider that keeps refusing says so.** A turn that gets only refusals, such as a rate limit, ended up silent for as long as fxbot ran. After 5 minutes it now stops, with the reason in the chat. A slow model that refuses nothing isn't touched.
- **More bots can work at once.** Up to 32 turns run together, up from 4, so four long commands no longer stop your other bots. **Turns at once** in **Settings** › **General** changes it, from the next time fxbot starts.
- **Routines,** from the new routines plugin, on fxbot's list. Ask a bot "every weekday at 8, summarize my calendar", and it saves a routine, which runs on time in a session of its own with everything the bot has: its computer, MCP servers, skills, memories, and library. The first release.

- **Bots and chats.** Bots drawn as animated blobs, each with a streaming chat that survives reloads and restarts, image and text attachments, and a `chat_history` tool that searches its own past.
- **Providers.** ChatGPT, Grok, and Vercel accounts by device code, API keys, and local model servers, with every key and token sealed in Postgres.
- **The library.** Every file the chats hold, searchable, for people and bots.
- **An installable app** for phones and desktops.
- **MCP servers.** Tools from any remote MCP server, signed in with OAuth or a key, for all bots or chosen ones, with only the tools you allow.
- **Releases.** `setup.sh` installs a release, and Settings or `npm run update` moves to the next one. The database migrates forward, with a backup first.
